Islamic finance guide

Practical reminders for organizing money in line with Islamic principles.
This guide is educational, not a fatwa. Details can differ between schools of thought and local scholars, so ask a trusted qualified scholar for a personal ruling.
Zakat al-mal

Qualifying monetary savings are commonly subject to 2.5% Zakat after reaching nisab and remaining above it for one lunar year (hawl). Use the Zakat calculator on the Savings page.

Nisab

Nisab is commonly valued using 85 grams of gold or 595 grams of silver. The value changes with the local market, so use a current local price and follow the basis advised for you.

Debt and obligations

Debt treatment can differ. The calculator allows immediate eligible debts to be entered, but only include debts your trusted scholar considers deductible.

Riba and ethical money

Riba generally refers to prohibited interest or unjustified increase in lending. Avoid interest-based arrangements where possible and seek qualified advice for contracts, loans, and accounts.

Sadaqah and responsible planning

Sadaqah is voluntary charity. Planning a regular amount, meeting essential needs, paying obligations, and avoiding waste can help turn a budget into a responsible financial habit.
